# 0030. A business model and impact projections, labelled "Projection, not results"

**Status:** Accepted, implemented.

## Context

Step 9, expanded. Judges score Business impact and the Problem, and so far
the site showed one worked example (2,000 employees × $50 a month) and no
account of how Local Goods itself would pay for itself. We wanted a model a
judge (or an adviser) can check line by line:
- three ways to grow: (A) the Golden Triangle only, (B) DC-wide and
  (C) a national rollout;
- every number traceable to a stated assumption with a source, or labelled
  "Assumption: to validate in customer discovery";
- nothing presented as a result. Local Goods has not run a pilot.

A 2013 prepaid-card model shared by a mentor was the guideline. It sold
employer cards through BIDs, earned mostly a per-card employer fee, paid the
BID per card, and assumed no payroll. We used its shape, not its numbers, and
neither name it nor include it.

It had to leave the rest alone:
- money logic, the ledger, Stripe, `MerchantFee`, `PlatformFee`;
- the fraud prompt (`8d5e1e70`) and AI input;
- eval data and the locks.

No migration, and no database is touched.

## Decision

**One source of truth.** `config/projections.php` holds every assumption.
Each one has:
- a low, base and high value, and a unit;
- a source link, or the assumption label;
- a note, and the date it was checked.

The facts that were checked on 2026-10-28:
- Stripe card, ACH and Connect prices;
- Claude Opus 5.5 token prices;
- DSLBD's DC BID count (12, though its own list names 13);
- the Golden Triangle's size;
- the 2010 count of about 1,000 U.S. BIDs;
- third-party estimates of benefit-platform fees ($4 to $12 per employee
  per month).

Everything else is labelled an assumption.

**A pure calculator.** `App\Support\Projections\BusinessModel` works month by
month for 72 months, and nothing in `app/Services` calls it (a test scans
both ways):
- **No live data:** no database, no network, and it never reads the live
  fee settings, the ledger or Stripe.
- **Growth:** BIDs launch on each scenario's curve; employers and merchants
  enroll in a straight line.
- **Revenue:** four separate lines that can each be 0: the BID initiation
  fee, the employer platform fee per participating employee per month, the
  merchant fee (a scenario rate; live stays 0) and interchange (only in a
  card-issuing phase, off).
- **Costs:**
  - Stripe funding (ACH or card) and Connect (active accounts, payouts);
  - one Claude API fraud check per redemption, from estimated tokens ×
    price;
  - hosting and the BID partner's revenue share;
  - support, sales and marketing, G&A with legal;
  - payroll that rises in steps.
- **Outputs (`Projection`):**
  - the P&L for 4 years;
  - monthly break-even (the first month from which EBITDA stays above 0, up
    to month 72) and peak cash needed;
  - unit economics, district impact, and customer funds.
- **What it takes:** for one BID at full ramp, the participating employees
  (and employers) needed to break even at the current fees, and the employer
  fee needed at the assumed participation. Both are searched rather than
  solved, because payroll steps.

The low/base/high case moves only demand and pricing. Costs stay fixed, so
revenue, spending and visits only rise from low to high.

**Customer money is not revenue.** Credits pass through to merchants. Unspent
balances and payouts owed are "customer funds held, a promise to pay", shown
on their own. Unspent credits go back to the employer: breakage is never
revenue, by policy, and a test checks revenue doesn't move with the share
spent.

**Where it shows.**
- **`/projection`** (public, linked from the landing page and the BID impact
  page): district numbers only, by scenario and case, with the labelled
  outside estimate. No fee, revenue or profit.
- **`/business-model`** and its CSV: signed-in reviewers, BID admin and
  platform admin. Employees, employers, merchants and logged-out visitors
  are refused, and personal contacts stay limited to adjudication. It shows
  the full P&L, charts with text tables, What it takes, the assumptions with
  sources, and the reference model's lessons. Every screen says "Projection,
  not results".
- **README `## Business model`:** a few sentences and a base-case table of
  revenue and EBITDA.
- **`docs/finance/MODEL.md`:** the method, every assumption, the differences
  from the reference, and what customer discovery must check.

The README table and the MODEL.md figures are generated by
`php artisan projections:docs`; `BusinessModelDocsTest` fails when they drift.
The three go-to-market costs (account managers per BID, sales cost per new
employer, BID launch cost) are named keys marked "Under review".

**The public-claims rule.** `PublicClaimsTest` now also catches revenue,
profit and EBITDA figures. They are allowed in only two places, each only
with "Projection, not results" and the assumptions link:
- the Business model page, as the judge;
- the README's Business model section.

Every page a logged-out visitor can see still has none. This is a deliberate
exception to "judges count as the public" (ADR 0029): judges see the
business model's projected fees, never real fee figures.

## Consequences

- **Base case (2026-10-28):**
  - The Golden Triangle alone never breaks even.
  - DC-wide breaks even in month 56, and national in month 50.
  - Peak cash needed is about $1.1M (DC-wide) and $3.5M (national).
  - One BID needs about 5,341 participating employees at a $6 fee, or a
    $13.54 fee at 1,200 employees.

  This is honest, and it says what discovery must test first: the employer
  fee and participation.
- The model is simple on purpose: straight-line enrollment, one purchase per
  employer per month, no churn, no seasonality, no taxes or interest. Its
  doc says so.
- Changing an assumption is one config edit plus `projections:docs`. The
  pages, the CSV and the docs all follow.
- The live platform fee (ADR 0021) is a percent and off. The model's
  per-employee fee is a model lever only and changes nothing in the product.
